What it does

Tacit runs on your computer and, if you want, your phone. When its camera recognises you coming back to your desk, it assembles a short spoken briefing and reads it out. Nobody else's face triggers it.

The rest of the time it stays out of the way. Say the wake word and ask it something; it answers using a reasoning model running on your own hardware.

Why it is private, in a way you can check

There is no Tacit server. There is nothing to sign up for. Nothing you say, read or do reaches us, because there is nowhere for it to go.

That is an architectural claim, not a promise, and it is worth being specific about what backs it:

  • The face model, the speech, the transcription and the reasoning model all run on your machine. Not a hosted API with a privacy policy — on your machine.
  • Camera frames become numbers and are deleted within about a second. A template is stored; an image never is.
  • Tacit recognises you. It stops at the first confident match and never tries to identify anyone else who walks past.
  • Your licence is verified offline by a signed file. Tacit does not phone home to check it, which means it keeps working if we vanish.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting. We cannot tell how many people use this.

Face recognition is off until you give written consent inside the application, and can be withdrawn at any time. See the biometric policy for exactly what is kept and for how long.

How it is built

One Flutter codebase compiled for Windows and Android, with a local helper process for the models. Face recognition uses ArcFace on the desktop and MobileFaceNet on Android. Reasoning is a quantised model through llama.cpp. Speech is neural and local.
